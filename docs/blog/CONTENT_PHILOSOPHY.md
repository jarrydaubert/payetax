# TaxInsights Content Philosophy

TaxInsights exists to make UK pay and tax easier to understand. It supports PayeTax's calculator and helps readers make sense of their payslips, salary decisions and relevant tax changes. **It is not a growth-content machine or a substitute for personalised tax advice.**

## Reader promise

**Your pay. Your money. Explained.**

We write for UK working adults who want a clear answer without a background in finance. They arrive with questions, not as members of a marketing generation. Treat them as capable, busy people; do not talk down to them.

Every article should answer:

1. **What's the answer?** Give the useful point early, without making readers search through definitions or caveats.
2. **Why is it true, and what does it mean for me?** Explain the rule clearly, with a concrete example when it helps.
3. **What can I do or check next?** Give a proportionate next step or a real PayeTax tool where relevant.

The format, depth and length follow the reader's question; an obligatory “Quick Summary”, worked character, FAQ or exact word count is not the editorial goal.

## The house voice

Knowledgeable, natural, warm, direct and quietly confident. Use contemporary British English without forced slang, hype or bureaucracy. A good article should sound like someone who understands payroll explaining it clearly, not a compliance circular or an advert.

**Accuracy and readability are both quality requirements.** Our source hierarchy and independent calculation evidence establish trust; clear, specific prose makes that evidence useful. Reviewers must protect material caveats **and** remove repetitive defensive phrasing.

See [VOICE_AND_STYLE.md](VOICE_AND_STYLE.md) for the positive writing brief, examples, five flexible formats and the single combined accuracy/readability review.

## Principles

- **Real value:** remove a confusion, answer a question or support a useful decision. No page exists solely to catch keywords.
- **Answer-first:** make the important point and its consequence clear near the beginning; use subsequent sections to add insight rather than repeat the summary.
- **Concrete:** show money in relatable pay periods and explain how the numbers were checked. Do not treat the calculator's own output as independent proof.
- **Trust-first:** verify changing rates and dates against canonical policy and current official sources; distinguish law, proposals and uncertainty.
- **Honest scope:** say when an example depends on region, tax year, student-loan plan, pension treatment or another material assumption. PayeTax is not a complete Self Assessment model.
- **Useful actions:** point to existing calculators, official guidance or relevant reading where it genuinely helps; avoid repeated marketing calls to action.
- **Consistent visual identity:** preserve the Ledger editorial image style without letting decorative consistency dictate article content.

## Guardrails

- No sensational “tax traps”, invented emergencies, guaranteed savings or implied regulatory endorsement.
- No filler, formulaic “let's dive in”, forced humour, memes or repetitive disclaimer language.
- No unverified provider, policy, product, security, production, readership or performance claims.
- No unsupported calculator scenarios or invented named reader stories passed off as real.
- No silent rewrites of historical articles as if their rates applied today.
- No invented editorial staff or named credentials. Attribution changes require a separate, truthful decision.

For rates, source hierarchy, tax-year discipline, metadata and publishing tests, follow [BLOG_GUIDE.md](BLOG_GUIDE.md). Editorial warmth never overrides statutory correctness.
