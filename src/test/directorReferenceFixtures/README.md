# Director Intelligence financial references

These five fictional companies cover decisions raised by the commission recruiter case, [JGF-37 committed cash](https://linear.app/jgf-projects/issue/JGF-37) and [JGF-166 year-end discovery](https://linear.app/jgf-projects/issue/JGF-166). ## Common basis

- England. UK trading company with one ordinary shareholder who is its sole director and employee. Where present, salary is paid in the 2026/27 personal tax year (6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027) and in the stated company period. Assume full-year category A director NI and no Employment Allowance.
- The company period is 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027 unless stated. The director has the standard Personal Allowance subject to taper. No other personal income, benefits, student loans, prior salary, dividends, drawings, associated companies, brought-forward losses, company distributions received or other adjustments except as stated. Revenue excludes VAT. Ordinary costs are allowable and exclude director salary, employer NI and any pension supplied separately.
- For annual comparisons that distribute the residual, assume sufficient funds, realised profits supported by relevant accounts and valid distributions in the stated tax year. If relying on interim accounts to distribute the period's profit, assume those accounts support the dividend by the relevant payment date. Dates of payment or entitlement determine the actual personal tax year.
- Keep pence in inputs and use annual earnings-period director NI. Use the exact percentage method, nearest penny with half a penny disregarded. The selected NI amounts do not have a half-penny tie. For these planning figures, round CT to the nearest penny at the end of the calculation, with exact half-penny ties rounded up. This is a planning convention. [HMRC's CT return guidance](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/cotax-manual/com130040) describes a separate filing basis. Use HMRC's whole-pound allowance taper and truncate each personal tax band's charge to pence on the given pence inputs. Browser strategy summaries display rounded pounds. The detailed workings and budgeting amounts show pence. Monthly draw results can display pence.
- CT means Corporation Tax. These are annual planning comparisons, not payroll withholdings, filed tax figures, distribution authorisation or proof of cash available. The initial executable cases cover the active Director tax year and England. Existing tax-year, regional and statutory boundary tests still own broader coverage.

The following sources establish the common basis. [HMRC 2026/27 rates for employers](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/rates-and-thresholds-for-employers-2026-to-2027), [CA44 director NI](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ca44-national-insurance-for-company-directors), [single-director Employment Allowance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/employment-allowance-more-detailed-guidance/single-director-companies-and-employment-allowance-further-employer-guidance), [rates on dividends](https://www.gov.uk/tax-on-dividends), [HMRC allowance and taper rounding](https://developer.service.hmrc.gov.uk/guides/tax-logic-service-guide/documentation/ty2627/allowances-and-reliefs.html), [HMRC band allocation and per-band rounding](https://developer.service.hmrc.gov.uk/guides/tax-logic-service-guide/documentation/ty2627/tax-calculation.html), [Corporation Tax rates](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/rates-and-allowances-corporation-tax/rates-and-allowances-corporation-tax), [Companies Act 2006 ss830 and 836](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/46/part/23), and [dividend timing](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/savings-and-investment-manual/saim5040).

## A · Commission recruiter

**Question.** How much salary can I fund in a quiet year, and what do I give up by committing to a smaller regular salary?

Sales of £11,400 less £3,120 ordinary costs leave £8,280. At salaries above £5,000, the company cost is salary + 15% × (salary − £5,000). At the maximum affordable salary, £7,852.17 plus £427.83 employer NI exactly uses the budget. The next penny exceeds it. There is no other tax, no dividend and no retained current profit.

At £6,708 salary (the annual Lower Earnings Limit), £256.20 employer NI leaves £1,315.80 taxable. CT of £250.00 (19% × £1,315.80) leaves £1,065.80 as a gross dividend. In All Dividends, CT of £1,573.20 leaves a £6,706.80 dividend. Neither strategy incurs personal tax. The LEL setup gives up £78.37 in annual take-home against All Salary and reduces regular gross salary by about £95.35 a month if spread evenly. The actual qualifying year depends on the director's NI record and payroll facts. Monthly cash and funding are outside this scenario. [What counts toward a qualifying year](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/your-new-state-pension-explained/your-state-pension-explained)

## B · Profitable consultancy

**Question.** Which salary/dividend option leaves the most cash, and what changes with another job, a pension payment or a prior trading loss?

£88,000 revenue less £8,000 costs leaves £80,000. One other qualifying associated company throughout the period halves the CT limits to £25,000 and £125,000. Augmented and taxable profits are equal here. Within these limits, use 25% × taxable profit less 3/200 × (£125,000 − taxable profit). [HMRC marginal relief formula](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/company-taxation-manual/ctm03925) · [Associated companies and thresholds](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/company-taxation-manual/ctm03935)

- **All Salary.** (£80,000 + £750) ÷ 1.15 funds £70,217.39 of salary and £9,782.61 of employer NI. Employee NI is 8% × £37,700 + 2% × £19,947.39 = £3,414.95. Income Tax is £7,540 plus £7,978.95 after truncating the 40% band. CT and dividends are zero.
- **Baseline Mix.** £12,570 salary plus £1,135.50 employer NI leaves £66,294.50 taxable, CT £15,693.04 and gross dividends £50,601.46. Salary uses the Personal Allowance. Dividend tax is 10.75% on £37,200 and 35.75% on £12,901.46 after allowing the £500 nil-rate band. Total personal tax is £8,611.27. This gives the most immediate cash of the three named options.
- **All Dividends.** £80,000 taxable profit gives CT £19,325.00 and dividends £60,675.00. After the £12,570 Personal Allowance and £500 nil-rate band, dividend tax is £3,999.00 plus £3,719.78 on £10,405 (35.75%, truncated).
- **Other employment.** use £12,570 salary and £40,000 unrelated PAYE income in the same personal year. Company results match Baseline Mix. Adjusted net income £103,171.46 reduces the Personal Allowance to £10,985. Non-dividend tax is £9,094, or £3,608 attributable to this comparison after subtracting £5,486 on the external job alone. Higher-band dividend tax is 35.75% on £50,101.46 = £17,911.27. Exclude the external wage and NI from director take-home. Payroll withholding and the balance owed require tax codes, records and amounts already paid.
- **Employer pension.** maintain the same director salary and external job. Pay £10,000 to a registered DC scheme in the company period and personal tax year, deductible wholly and exclusively, with no other pension inputs, reduced allowance or spreading. Taxable profit falls to £56,294.50, CT to £13,043.04 and dividends to £43,251.46. Adjusted net income £95,821.46 leaves the full allowance. Incremental non-dividend tax is £8,460 − £5,486 = £2,974. Dividend tax is 35.75% on £42,751.46 = £15,283.64 after truncation. Cash falls £4,088.37 against the external-job case and £10,000 goes into pension. [Deduction timing](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/business-income-manual/bim46010) · [Trade-purpose test](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/business-income-manual/bim46035) · [Annual allowance](https://www.gov.uk/tax-on-your-private-pension/annual-allowance)
- **Carried-forward loss.** All Dividends with a valid claim for £20,000 post-2017 eligible trading loss under the deductions allowance. The prior tax loss came from a book–tax difference. Opening realised reserves are independently established as zero, with no deferred-tax movement. £60,000 taxable profit gives £14,025.00 CT. Accounting profit before CT remains £80,000, so the residual dividend is £65,975.00. Dividend tax is £3,999.00 plus £5,614.53 on £15,705. The amount of loss relief alone establishes neither cash nor realised reserves. [Trading loss relief](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/corporation-tax-calculating-and-claiming-a-loss)

## C · Contractor with committed cash

**Question.** Can I draw £1,000 this month and still meet commitments and preserve runway?

Start in April. Use £1,600 monthly contract income and £300 ordinary monthly costs through March. Bank cash is a current snapshot. Extra unpaid commitments sit outside the forecast tax and buffered costs. Use three months of £300 ordinary costs plus £1,000 target draw each month, or £3,900 in operating buffer. The dates and inputs describe a planning assumption. They do not establish income already earned, dates of receipt, assessed tax or the legality of a distribution.

- **All Dividends.** £19,200 − £3,600 = £15,600 projected profit and £2,964 CT at 19%. £12,636 dividends include £12,570 covered by the Personal Allowance and £66 covered by the dividend nil-rate band. The forecast-based cap is £1,053. Deduct forecast CT, separate commitments and the £3,900 buffer once each to establish positive headroom. Draw is the lower of headroom and £1,053. The £1,000 target measures the spending gap.
- With £9,000 cash and blank commitments, headroom is £2,136, so draw is £1,053. A £1,500 commitment cuts both headroom and draw to £636, £364 below the target. £2,136 exhausts the draw headroom and leaves the operating buffer on track. £2,300 produces a £164 operating buffer shortfall. An explicit £0 and a blank commitment give equal draw figures with different disclosures.
- **Payroll reserve check.** For the same forecast choose Baseline Mix and £5,000 cash, with extra commitments blank. £12,570 salary and £1,135.50 employer NI leave £1,894.50 taxable. CT at 19% is £359.955 before rounding, or £359.96 under the planning convention. Dividend tax is 10.75% × (£1,534.54 − £500), truncated to £111.21. Forecast take-home is £13,993.33 and the monthly cap is £1,166.11. Reserving CT and employer NI leaves £3,504.54 cash, £395.46 short of the buffer. Draw is £0, so the entire £1,000 spending gap remains. This carries forward the older monthly recruiter test's independent proof that employer NI reduces the funded buffer.

**C zero-bank funding gap.** The £3,900 shortfall is the operating buffer by design. The separate £2,964 projected CT reserve is also unfunded in this case. The result shows CT to set aside and does not quantify total unfunded reserves. [JGF-166](https://linear.app/jgf-projects/issue/JGF-166) owns whether to distinguish still-to-fund tax from projected tax once payment and allocation inputs can be established.

## D · Loss-making studio with prior reserves

**Question.** Does this year's loss rule out dividends?

£9,000 sales less £12,000 ordinary costs gives £3,000 accounting and trading loss before any director payroll. No current CT and no carry-back claim. Opening undistributed realised profits are £9,000. Illustrative closing balances before any distribution are £4,000 bank, a fully collectible £4,501 customer receivable due after the near-term liabilities, £2,500 of those liabilities already reflected in the accounts, and £1 share capital. Net assets of £6,001 reconcile to £6,000 realised profits plus share capital. Immediate cash over those liabilities is £1,500 before any other requirements, not a prudent distribution recommendation. Paying a £6,708 salary with £256.20 employer NI would increase the illustrative loss to £9,964.20 before relief and would require additional funding. [Distributions and relevant accounts](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/46/part/23)

**Open reference, manual reproduction.** Choose Annual and England, enter the revenue and expenses above and Calculate. Survival Mode shows the rounded £9,964 illustrative loss and concludes dividends are impossible. The product has no input for opening reserves. A current loss alone does not rule out a dividend. Relevant accounts, subsequent losses, obligations and solvency still matter. This is an explicitly unresolved case owned by [JGF-51](https://linear.app/jgf-projects/issue/JGF-51), not a passing statutory verification.

## E · First accounts ending in May

**Question.** How much CT should I reserve and which payments and returns fall due?

Incorporate and start trading on 11 May 2025. Draft first accounts end 31 May 2026. Turnover £83,200 less allowable costs £6,000 gives taxable trading profit £77,200 with no remuneration, distributions, adjustments or instalments. Assume no notice timing exception, no filed returns or tax payments, and a day split representative of the trade. This is one set of accounts with two CT periods.

- £77,200 over 386 days is £200 per day. 11 May 2025–10 May 2026 has £73,000 taxable profits and retains the full thresholds. At unchanged rates across financial years 2025 and 2026, tax is £18,250 less £2,655 marginal relief, or £15,595.
- 11–31 May 2026 has £4,200 taxable profits. Prorate both thresholds by 21/365, about £2,876.71 and £14,383.56. Keep full precision until final CT. £1,050 less 3/200 × (£250,000 × 21/365 − £4,200) gives £897.25 at pence precision. Reserve £16,492.25 across the periods. Payments are due 11 February and 1 March 2027 using separate references. Both returns are due 31 May 2027 under the long-accounts rule. First accounts are due at Companies House on 11 February 2027. [First accounts and returns](https://www.gov.uk/first-company-accounts-and-return/trading-from-set-up) · [Apportionment](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/company-taxation-manual/ctm01405) · [Short-period limits](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/company-taxation-manual/ctm03930) · [Payment dates](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/company-taxation-manual/ctm01800) · [Long-accounts return deadline](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/company-taxation-manual/ctm93030) · [Accounts filing deadline](https://www.gov.uk/annual-accounts) · [Payment references](https://www.gov.uk/pay-corporation-tax/bank-or-building-society)

**Open reference, manual reproduction.** choose Annual, England and All Dividends to expose CT before any salary. Enter the full turnover, costs and 31 May 2026 end date, then Calculate. The product uses full-year thresholds and displays £16,708. Change only the date to 10 May and Calculate. CT remains £16,708 and the standard calendar moves to 11 February 2027 payment and 10 May 2027 filing. Enter the stub's net £4,200 as revenue with zero costs and restore 31 May 2026 to reproduce £798 CT. The date input cannot model a period start or two CT periods. No dividends were actually paid in this scenario. Draft figures, filed amounts, HMRC credit allocations and bank payments need separate evidence.
