The Budget on **28 October 2026** leaves headline employment Income Tax and NI rates unchanged for 2027/28. Most of the tax changes affect income outside your payslip: savings interest, rental profit and small side earnings.

**If you only read one thing:** headline employment tax rates stay the same, but a pay rise can increase deductions. Main NI payment thresholds remain frozen; the Lower Earnings Limit (LEL) rises. Devolved Income Tax decisions are separate.

## Budget 2026 at a glance

On a phone, swipe tables sideways to see every column.

| Who is affected | Headline change | From tax year / date |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Employees | Rates retained; LEL rises | 2027/28 |
| Minimum-wage workers | **£13.21** (21+); **£11.27** (18–20) | 1 April 2027 |
| Own-car business mileage | **25p to 35p** above 10,000 miles | 6 April 2027 |
| Savers | Proposed **24% / 44% / 49%** | 6 April 2027 |
| Landlords | Proposed **24% / 44% / 49%** | 6 April 2027 |
| Side income | Proposed **£3,000** allowance | 6 April 2027 |
| Cash ISA savers | **£20,000** limit retained | April 2027 |
| Families | **£28.00** eldest; **£18.55** others | 2027/28 |

Wages are hourly; Child Benefit amounts are weekly. Proposed property rates cover England, Wales and Northern Ireland. The savings, property and trading proposals need Finance Bill 2026–27; the ISA, mileage and NI uprating changes use regulations. The mileage row covers approved own-car/van business miles above 10,000, announced on **27 October 2026**, before Budget day ([OOTLAR 2.7](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/budget-2026-overview-of-tax-legislation-and-rates-ootlar/budget-2026-overview-of-tax-legislation-and-rates-ootlar)).

## Your payslip: what stays the same

For 2027/28, [HMRC's rates and allowances](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/budget-2026-overview-of-tax-legislation-and-rates-ootlar/annex-a-rates-and-allowances) show:

- **Personal Allowance:** £12,570, unchanged.
- **Main Income Tax rates:** 20%, 40% and 45%, with the same band limits.
- **Employee National Insurance:** 8% between the payment thresholds and 2% above.
- **Employer National Insurance:** 15%.

Budget 2025's Personal Allowance and basic-rate-limit freeze through 2030/31 was enacted in Finance Act 2026. Main NI payment thresholds are retained, but the **Lower Earnings Limit rises from £129 to £133 a week**. It helps determine contribution records and benefit entitlement, rather than when employees start paying NI. Other NI upratings also apply ([OOTLAR 2.1](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/budget-2026-overview-of-tax-legislation-and-rates-ootlar/budget-2026-overview-of-tax-legislation-and-rates-ootlar)).

Rising wages against frozen bands create **fiscal drag**. Scotland sets its salary Income Tax rates and bands; Wales sets devolved rates. The UK Budget alone does not settle those 2027/28 decisions.

## The National Living Wage rises to £13.21

From **1 April 2027**, the National Living Wage for workers aged 21 and over rises to **£13.21 an hour**, from £12.71. The 18–20 minimum rate rises to £11.27 ([official minimum wage rates](https://www.gov.uk/national-minimum-wage-rates)). The new rate applies from the first pay reference period starting on or after the change ([minimum wage timing rules](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/calculating-the-minimum-wage/calculating-the-minimum-wage)).

**Example:** someone in England aged 21 or over, working 37.5 paid hours a week for 52 weeks, with tax code **1257L M1** (an emergency basis that taxes each month separately), NI category A, no pension, student loan or other deductions:

| Pay measure | 2026/27 | 2027/28 scenario | Increase |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gross pay a year | £24,784.50 | £25,759.50 | +£975.00 |
| Annualised take-home | £21,367.02 | £22,069.62 | **+£702.60** |
| Average monthly take-home | £1,780.59 | £1,839.14 | +£58.55 |

**That is £702.60 more a year, or about £58.55 a month, in this illustration.** Roughly 72p of each extra pound remains after 20% Income Tax and 8% employee NI.

**Payroll basis:** a steady-pay Month 1 illustration using [2026/27 HMRC routines](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/payroll-technical-specifications-income-tax): £1,048.26 of monthly pay covered by the tax code, with taxable pay rounded down to whole pounds; NI rounded separately above its [£1,048 monthly threshold](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/rates-and-thresholds-for-employers-2026-to-2027). The 2027/28 column substitutes the announced wage and retained rates and annual/weekly thresholds, using existing monthly routines. It is **not a supported 2027/28 calculator result or cumulative payroll forecast**. Monthly figures are rounded annual averages, so multiplying them by 12 can differ by 6p. Final payroll guidance or different deductions can change outcomes.

## Savings and rental income: proposed higher rates from April 2027

| Income Tax band | 2026/27 savings | Enacted 2027/28 | Proposed 2027/28 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Basic | 20% | 22% | 24% |
| Higher | 40% | 42% | 44% |
| Additional | 45% | 47% | 49% |

The **22%, 42% and 47% rates announced in Budget 2025 are enacted law** for 6 April 2027 ([Finance Act 2026, sections 5–8](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2026/11/contents/enacted)). Budget 2026 **proposes** another two percentage points through Finance Bill 2026–27. The enacted rates remain law unless amended.

- **Savings rates** apply across the UK.
- **Property rates** are published for England, Wales and Northern Ireland. Scotland and Wales have powers to set their own: check devolved decisions.
- **They don't touch your salary.** These rates apply to savings interest and rental profit, not employment income.

What stays the same:

- **Personal Savings Allowance:** £1,000 for basic-rate taxpayers, £500 for higher-rate taxpayers, none for additional-rate taxpayers.
- **Starting rate for savings:** up to £5,000, depending on your other income.
- **ISA interest:** remains tax-free.

**Example:** a higher-rate taxpayer earns £1,000 interest outside an ISA, with no starting-rate relief. After the £500 allowance, £500 is taxable, all in the higher-rate band. Tax is **£200** in 2026/27, **£210** under the enacted 2027/28 rates, or **£220** if the 2026 proposal becomes law. Our [Budget 2025 article](/blog/autumn-budget-2025-uk-tax-changes-explained) has a rental-profit example.

## Side income and ISAs

**Trading allowance: proposed increase from £1,000 to £3,000.** Subject to the Finance Bill, from 6 April 2027 eligible gross trading and miscellaneous income up to £3,000 could be covered, alongside a higher trading-income reporting threshold. For example, £2,500 from paid gardening, with no other trading or miscellaneous income, could fall within the proposed allowance. It is not an extra allowance on salary. Above the limit, eligible traders can choose the allowance or actual expenses, not both.

**Selling unwanted personal possessions is usually not trading.** Buying or making items to sell for profit can be trading; clearing out your own old clothes or furniture normally is not. Separate rules can apply to valuable possessions. See [HMRC's selling-online guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/selling-goods-or-services-on-a-digital-platform).

**Cash ISA: limit stays at £20,000.** A previously planned cut is reversed. It is part of the overall £20,000 ISA allowance, not on top of it.

**Lifetime ISA:** for a first home costing more than £450,000, the withdrawal charge is to fall from 25% to 20% from 29 October 2026 through regulations. Other unauthorised withdrawals retain the 25% charge. Check with your provider first.

## Announced earlier, still coming

These policies have Budget 2025 origins. Budget 2026 provides implementation detail for the Pension Tax Allowance; the protection itself was previously announced.

| Earlier announcement | What it means | From |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pension salary sacrifice | NI above **£2,000/year** | 6 April 2029 |
| Pension Tax Allowance | Proposed small-bill relief | 6 April 2027 to 5 April 2030 |
| Plan 2 student loans | **£29,385** threshold freeze | 2027/28 to 2029/30 |

The salary-sacrifice cap affects employee and employer NI on sacrificed pay. Income Tax relief is unchanged; this is not a pension-contribution limit. The primary framework is enacted; operating rules require regulations.

The proposed Finance Bill Pension Tax Allowance covers small tax bills where pensioner income is no more than the full new State Pension, tapering above that level. A pay rise can increase Plan 2 repayments while its threshold is frozen.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Budget 2026 change the tax on my salary?

The UK Budget retains the main employment tax rates and bands and employee NI rates. Main NI payment thresholds are retained, but the Lower Earnings Limit rises. Check devolved Income Tax decisions for Scotland and Wales. Your deductions can still rise if your pay rises.

### Will I pay more tax on my savings?

Enacted law sets savings rates at **22%, 42% and 47%** from 6 April 2027. Budget 2026 proposes **24%, 44% and 49%** instead. These apply to taxable interest above your allowances, outside an ISA. Many savers pay no tax because of the Personal Savings Allowance and starting rate for savings.

### Is every Budget announcement already law?

No. The savings, property and trading allowance proposals need Parliament to pass the Finance Bill. The ISA and mileage changes use regulations. The salary-sacrifice primary framework is already enacted, with operating detail left to regulations. Check final rules before making decisions based on a proposal.
