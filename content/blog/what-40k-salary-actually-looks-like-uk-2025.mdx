For 2026/27, PayeTax estimates that a £40,000 annual salary produces take-home pay of about **£32,322 a year**, or **£2,694 a month**.

That is a modelled PAYE result, not a statement of exactly what every employee will receive.

## Assumptions

The estimate uses:

- England, Wales or Northern Ireland Income Tax
- tax code [1257L](https://www.gov.uk/tax-codes/what-your-tax-code-means) for England and Northern Ireland or `C1257L` for Wales
- 12 equal monthly pay periods
- a Month 1-style steady-pay projection repeated across those 12 periods, not a cumulative month-by-month payroll simulation
- employee National Insurance category A
- no pension contribution
- no Student Loan
- no benefits, other income or other payroll deductions

## £40,000 PAYE Estimate

| Basis | Gross pay | Income Tax | Employee NI | Estimated take-home |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 12 equal monthly periods | £40,000 | £5,484 | £2,194 | **£32,322** |
| One monthly period | £3,333 | £457 | £183 | **£2,694** |

The annual row is twelve modelled monthly payroll results added together. Actual deductions can differ because of tax-code basis, cumulative pay, irregular earnings, benefits or other payroll adjustments.

## Why a Simplified Annual Calculation Differs

Applying the published annual thresholds directly gives a slightly different illustration:

The Income Tax side illustrates annual liability under the stated assumptions. The NI side applies annualised thresholds only for comparison; ordinary employee NI is assessed by earnings period.

| Method | Income Tax | Employee NI | Take-home |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Monthly payroll model × 12 | £5,484.00 | £2,193.96 | £32,322.04 |
| Simplified annual illustration | £5,486.00 | £2,194.40 | £32,319.60 |

The annual Income Tax illustration subtracts the £12,570 Personal Allowance and charges 20% on the remaining £27,430. The annual NI illustration applies 8% above the £12,570 Primary Threshold.

PAYE and employee NI are operated by pay period. PayeTax therefore uses HMRC's monthly free-pay, band and NI thresholds, together with payroll rounding, for the headline estimate. The simplified annual calculation is useful for explaining the bands, but it is not a monthly payslip calculation.

## With a Plan 2 Student Loan

The [Plan 2 thresholds for 2026/27](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/special-rules-for-student-loans) are £29,385 annually, £2,448.75 monthly and £565.09 weekly, with deductions at 9% above the threshold for the pay period.

Using HMRC's [monthly deduction rules](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/sl3-student-loan-deduction-tables/2026-to-2027-student-and-postgraduate-loan-deduction-tables) for the same £40,000 example:

- monthly Plan 2 deduction: £79
- annual Plan 2 deduction across 12 equal monthly pay periods: £948
- estimated annual take-home after tax, NI and Plan 2: £31,374
- estimated monthly take-home: £2,615

Payroll rounds each Student Loan deduction down to a whole pound. Irregular pay or a different pay frequency can therefore change the annual total.

## With a 5% Salary Sacrifice

If 5% of the whole £40,000 salary is validly sacrificed in exchange for an employer pension contribution, with the other base assumptions unchanged including no Student Loan, PayeTax models:

- £2,000 contributed by the employer
- £38,000 remaining contractual cash pay
- estimated annual take-home: £30,883
- estimated monthly take-home: £2,574

This example is only for [salary sacrifice](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/salary-sacrifice-and-the-effects-on-paye). It is not a generic "5% workplace pension" result.

With [net pay](https://www.gov.uk/workplace-pensions/managing-your-pension), the employee contribution is deducted before Income Tax but does not reduce National Insurance. With relief at source, the contribution is taken after tax and NI, the provider adds basic-rate relief, and the contribution shown on the payslip is not the full gross amount added to the pension. A scheme may also calculate contributions from qualifying earnings rather than the whole salary.

## Is £40,000 Above Median Earnings?

The latest applicable [ONS Annual Survey of Hours and Earnings](https://www.ons.gov.uk/employmentandlabourmarket/peopleinwork/earningsandworkinghours/bulletins/annualsurveyofhoursandearnings/2025) reported median gross annual earnings of £39,039 in April 2025 for full-time employees who had been in their jobs for at least a year. The estimate is provisional and covers a defined employee group.

On that specific measure, £40,000 is £961 above the median. It does not establish a universal UK income percentile or whether the salary is sufficient for a particular household, location, rent, mortgage or savings target.

## If You Are a Scottish Taxpayer

Scottish taxpayers use different [Income Tax rates and bands](https://www.gov.uk/scottish-income-tax). Scottish taxpayer status normally depends on where you live, not where your employer is based.

Use the [Scottish tax calculator](/tools/scottish-tax-calculator) for a current comparison. Employee NI and Student Loan rules are separate from the Scottish Income Tax bands.

## What Can Change the Result?

Material differences can come from:

- a tax code other than 1257L or C1257L
- cumulative, Week 1, Month 1 or other non-cumulative tax-code operation
- bonuses, overtime or uneven pay
- pension method and contribution basis
- Student Loan plan and pay frequency
- benefits, taxable expenses or other income
- a different NI category or reaching State Pension age
- Scottish taxpayer status

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much is £40,000 after tax in 2026/27?

Under the assumptions in this article, PayeTax estimates about £32,322 annual take-home, or £2,694 a month, after Income Tax and employee National Insurance.

### Why does the annual illustration give a different result?

The headline estimate adds twelve monthly payroll calculations, including period thresholds and rounding. The separate £32,319.60 annual illustration applies annual thresholds directly, so it should not be divided by 12 and treated as a payslip result.

### What is the Plan 2 take-home estimate at £40,000?

With the same assumptions plus a Plan 2 loan, the estimate is about £31,374 a year or £2,615 a month. Irregular pay can change deductions because payroll uses period thresholds and rounds each deduction down to a whole pound.

### Does a 5% pension always produce the figure shown here?

No. The figure shown is for 5% of the whole salary under salary sacrifice. Net pay, relief at source and contributions based on qualifying earnings produce different payslip results.

### Is £40,000 above the UK median salary?

It is slightly above the ONS April 2025 median gross annual earnings figure of £39,039 for full-time employees who had been in their jobs for at least a year. That specific comparison does not establish a universal earnings percentile or household living standard.
